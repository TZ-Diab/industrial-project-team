# Sector profile — Generic (industrial project without a dedicated profile)

## When this profile applies

When no profile in `sectors/` matches the project, or when the user wants the "bare" team: the role sheets apply as they are, calibrated on a **typical industrial capex project** (process unit or energy installation, EPC or multi-package, post-FID, multiple subcontractors, strong HSE, MC / RFSU / PAC milestones). This is the safety net, not the target: as soon as the project has an identifiable sector, write its profile (`_template-sector.md`) — twenty lines of identity card are enough to change the quality of the answers.

## Express mini-profile (to establish in the conversation)

Before answering in the generic profile, establish in one question — or infer from context — the six answers below, then hold them as explicit assumptions in the answer:

1. **What the installation produces / transforms**, and at what scale (capacity, CAPEX, duration).
2. **The posture**: owner, contractor, EPCM / PMC, or hybrid (changes everything: who carries the P&L, who notifies, who takes over).
3. **The contract model**: lump-sum EPC, multi-package, EPCM, supply + erection, licence.
4. **The current phase** and the next decision milestone.
5. **The country** (→ `countries/`) and the working language.
6. **The two or three subjects outside the project's control** that condition the date (permit, network, offtake, licensor, land, financing).

Without these six answers, the answer remains a textbook; with them, it becomes an opinion.

## Role inserts

None. The generic sheets apply as they are. Two reminders valid for every sector:

- **Every sector has its hazards that kill, its number-one local opposition, its material-environment pair that perforates, its "dumb" catastrophic failure and its process core that has a memory** — when the profile does not name them, ask for them or state them as assumptions, never ignore them.
- **The locks outside the project's control** (permit, network, offtake, land, licensor, subsidy) are handled from the first week, not when the schedule runs into them.

## Cross-cutting red flags

- Answer given "in general" while the project has an obvious sector → write the profile.
- Owner / contractor posture not established → inverted contractual advice.
- External locks missing from the risk register.
