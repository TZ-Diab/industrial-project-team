# Sector profile — Petrochemicals, refining, process chemistry (licensed units, brownfield / grassroots)

> **Example profile — unreviewed.** Written as an illustration of the expected density of a sector profile, from general industry knowledge, not from field practice validated by the author. Figures are indicative; regulatory references are given by family with examples, to be replaced by the project country's set (`countries/standards-regimes.md`). To use it: copy into `references/sectors/refining-petrochemicals.md`, then review every line with a practitioner.

## Sector identity card

- **Object**: **continuous chemical process** unit — refining (hydrotreating, reforming, cracking), petrochemicals (steam cracking, polymers, aromatics), base chemicals, gas treatment, biofuels and green chemistry — often under **process licence** (a licensor supplies the basic package), **grassroots** (new site) or, most often, **brownfield** (extension, revamp, debottlenecking of an operating site).
- **Typical scale**: from 20 M€ (revamp) to several billion (complex). FEL → start-up: 3 to 6 years; FEED 9–15 months; EPC 24–40 months. EPC teams of tens to hundreds of people, organised by complete disciplines — **the reference from which the generic sheets originate**.
- **Dominant contract models**: **licence + PDP** (process design package) by the licensor; **FEED** (reimbursable) then **lump-sum EPC** (conversion after FEED) or **reimbursable EPCM**; **PMC** on the Owner's side; EPC with **process guarantees** (yields, product quality, consumptions) carried back-to-back on the licensor; supply contracts for critical equipment (reactors, compressors) sometimes placed by the Owner (free issue).
- **Life cycle & gates**: `FEL 1 (concept, selection) → FEL 2 (pre-FEED, licensor choice) → FEL 3 (FEED, Class 2/3 estimate) → FID → DETAILED ENGINEERING → PROCUREMENT → CONSTRUCTION → PRE-COMM / COMM → START-UP → PERFORMANCE TEST RUN → PAC → FAC`. Front-end loading and design freeze condition everything (influence curve).
- **Regulatory framework and permits** (by family; the country decides the set): **major-hazard regime** upper / lower tier (Seveso III, COMAH, OSHA PSM + EPA RMP, MHF…) with hazard study / safety report, land-use planning constraints, on-site / off-site emergency plans; **integrated pollution regime** (IED and BREFs — REF, LVOC, CWW — with BAT conclusions in the EU; Title V / NSR in the US); **pressure equipment** (PED / ASME) with statutory in-service inspection and requalification; **hazardous areas**; CO₂ allowances (EU ETS and equivalents); aqueous and atmospheric discharges, water permits; labour law for co-activity on an operating site (prevention plan, permit-to-work system). Country cards: `countries/`.
- **Key external counterparts**: **licensor**, **site operator** (brownfield: operations, maintenance, inspection, site HSE), environmental and industrial-safety authority, inspection bodies (notified bodies, authorised inspectors), critical-equipment suppliers (long-lead), abnormal-load hauliers (heavy lift), site workers' safety committee.
- **FID locks / long poles**: **PDP delivered and frozen** (the licensor drives the upstream calendar), **long-lead equipment** (reactors, columns, compressors: 18–36 months), **major-hazard permit**, **turnaround window for the tie-ins** in brownfield (a hard date, often every 4–6 years: missing it = a year of delay), **soil studies and as-built data** on an existing site, resource market (welders, pipefitters).
- **Economics & revenue**: refining / petrochemical margin (crack spreads), material yield, **availability and reliability**, energy efficiency, CO₂ cost; in brownfield: **production losses** during the shutdown and the tie-ins often exceed the project CAPEX; balance-sheet financing more than project finance.
- **Roles to activate in addition**: **Licensing / process package engineer** and **Turnaround / tie-in coordinator** (below). Generic roles at full stretch; **grid & utility connection** becomes **utilities and OSBL** (steam, water, air, flare, site electricity); **financing & FID** often reduces to internal capex approval.
- **Phase skills and references available**: no dedicated phase skill; the cross-cutting EPC skills (claims / EOT, cost control, contractual letters and minutes, engineering register, specifications, multi-package reconciliation, annex simplification, project indexing, gate war-game, steering challenger) apply as they are — this is their sector of origin.
- **Common units & ratios** (indicative): t/yr, kt/yr, bbl/d; Lang factor ~3–5 (fluid process); equipment share ~25–35% of CAPEX, piping 15–25%; engineering hours per equipment item; **availability** 95–98%; **TAR** every 4–6 years, 4–8 weeks; performance guarantee window (test run 72 h to a few days); weld density (dia-inches) as the piping productivity unit.
- **Key vocabulary**: PDP / BEDP, licensor, ISBL / OSBL, tie-in, hot tap, turnaround (TAR), SIMOPS, plot plan, pipe rack, heavy lift, modularisation, HAZOP, LOPA, SIL, PSV / flare, RBI, HTHA, CUI, sulfidation, PWHT, PMI, hydrotest, catalyst loading, chemical cleaning, steam-out, N₂ purge, start-up, performance test run, punch list, MC / RFSU, major-hazard tier, land-use planning zone, emergency plans, BAT / BREF, permit to work, prevention plan.

## Sector-specific roles

### Licensing / process package engineer
- **Mission**: holds the interface with the **licensor** — obtain the PDP on time, freeze it, make the process guarantees hold and enforce the licence requirements (equipment, design, confidentiality) up to the performance test run.
- **Reading grid of a PDP**: (1) design basis and cases (feeds, products, capacities, turndown); (2) HMB and governing cases; (3) list of "licensor-critical" equipment and imposed suppliers; (4) material and metallurgy requirements; (5) guarantees (yields, quality, consumptions) and test-run conditions; (6) exclusions and battery limits; (7) licensor review and approval lead times on the detailed documents (often the bottleneck).
- **Red flags**: "final" PDP evolving after FID; guarantees conditioned on utilities the site does not supply; licensor review times not scheduled; catalyst not ordered (long-lead and cash).
- **Senior eye**: the licensor guarantees its process, not your unit — everything outside the PDP (utilities, OSBL, interfaces) is the EPC's and the Owner's risk.

### Turnaround / tie-in coordinator (brownfield)
- **Mission**: fit the project's works into the **site's shutdown window**: tie-in list, sequence, resources, permits, isolations, contingencies.
- **Grid**: (1) exhaustive tie-in list with method (shutdown, hot tap, bypass); (2) critical duration of each tie-in vs window; (3) works feasible **outside the shutdown** (prefabrication, pre-installation, SIMOPS); (4) isolation and lock-out plan validated by the operator; (5) fallback plan if the window slips.
- **Senior eye**: "everything that can be done before the shutdown is done before"; the shutdown window is the only project date that does not move — everything else bends to it.

## Role inserts

### Project manager
- Sector of origin of the generic sheets: they apply in full. Specifics: **design freeze** after FEED (late churn is the number-one cause of overrun), **licensor interface**, **brownfield** (SIMOPS, prevention plan, the operator's authority over its site), **shutdown window** as a hard date, **heavy lift** and abnormal logistics.
- Red flags: PDP not frozen at FID; tie-ins not listed before FEED; long-lead items not ordered before design freeze; Owner standards (site specs) discovered after award.

### Estimator
- **Equipment-factored** (Class 4) then **detailed by discipline** (Class 3/2); Lang / Hand factors by equipment type; escalation and currencies over 3–4 years; **brownfield**: degraded productivity factors (access, permits, SIMOPS), demolition, tie-ins, production losses; contingency by simulation (QCRA) rather than a percentage.
- Anchor: equipment 25–35%, piping 15–25%, civil / structures 10–15%, electrical / instrumentation 10–15%, erection / indirects the rest — indicative, to be recalibrated.

### Cost controller
- Full EVM, commitments on dozens of contracts and hundreds of orders; **vendor progress payments**; currency and escalation; change orders by the hundred (trend register); **cost of production losses** tracked separately; monthly re-forecast by discipline.

### Planner
- L4 schedule by discipline and by area; **typical critical path**: PDP → long-lead equipment → heavy foundations → column / reactor erection → piping → E&I → pre-comm; **TAR window** as a hard constraint; **heavy lift** (cranes, roads, transport permits); piping productivity in dia-inches; resources (qualified welders) as the real constraint.

### Contract manager
- Lump-sum EPC converted after FEED: contractual **process guarantees** (yields, quality, consumptions) and **performance test run**, performance and delay LDs, back-to-back with the licensor and the vendors; **brownfield**: responsibilities shared with the operator (as-built data, isolations, access), SIMOPS and site-induced delays = a major source of claims; Owner standards as contract documents.

### Risk manager
- Dominant risks: design churn, long-lead and vendor quality, **TAR window**, wrong brownfield data (as-built, contaminated soil, buried networks), resource market, process safety during the tie-ins, product market (margin), regulatory (IED, CO₂, land-use planning).

### Financing & FID
- Internal capex decision on a business case (margin, availability, CO₂) rather than project finance; sensitivity to production losses and the restart date; FEL gates with a Class 2/3 estimate required.

### Process engineer
- Full stretch: PFD / P&ID, HMB, **governing overpressure and flare cases**, control philosophy, HAZOP / LOPA, **licensor** (reviews), utilities (steam, water, air), effluents; brownfield: existing capacities (flare, utilities) often the real bottleneck of the debottlenecking.

### Mechanical engineer
- Reactors, columns, exchangers (TEMA), furnaces, **API 617 / 618 compressors**, API 610 pumps, critical rotating machines (spares, alignment, protection); thick walls, clad, PWHT; vendor shop inspection; heavy lift.

### Materials & corrosion engineer
- **The core of the selection**: H₂S / NACE MR0175 and ISO 15156, **HTHA** (Nelson curves), sulfidation (McConomy), naphthenic acids, **CUI**, stress corrosion cracking (chlorides, amines), high-temperature hydrogen; API 571 as the mechanism reference; corrosion allowance and clad materials; killer pair: **wrong grade in hot H₂ service** (delayed, catastrophic failure).

### Piping engineer
- Full stretch: piping classes, stress (B31.3, thermal and nozzle loads), **flare headers**, supports and pipe racks, isos by the thousand, MTO, **tie-ins and hot taps**, hydrotests; dia-inches as the steering unit.

### Civil / structural engineer
- Pipe racks, compressor foundations (DIN 4024 / API), column foundations (wind, seismic), **blast-resistant buildings** (control room, substations), bunds, buried networks in brownfield (discovering the existing = major uncertainty).

### Geotechnical engineer
- Brownfield: disturbed, contaminated soils, existing networks and foundations; campaigns constrained by permits to work; grassroots: heavy foundations and machine vibrations.

### Electrical engineer
- HV / MV, large motors and VFDs, substations, discrimination, extended **hazardous areas**, emergency and safety supply (UPS for SIS), interface with the existing site network (capacity, overall discrimination).

### Instrumentation engineer
- Dense instrumentation, **SIL 2–3 SIFs**, severe-service safety and control valves, online analysers, **fiscal / custody metering**, integration with the existing site.

### DCS / control systems engineer
- Integration with the **existing DCS** (or new), **alarm rationalisation**, separate SIS, Cause & Effect matrices, IEC 62443 cybersecurity, migration without shutdown in brownfield.

### HSE
- Hazards that kill: **SIMOPS** on an operating site, **hot work** near hydrocarbons, **confined spaces** (reactors, columns, H₂S), heavy lifts, falls; the operator's **permit to work** as the law of the site; process safety (KPIs, HAZOP, PSSR) on a par with occupational safety.

### Technical safety
- **QRA, major-hazard study, land-use planning**, F&G, passive protection (PFP), deluge, **flare and depressurisation**, SIL, layout; references API 521, NFPA, Owner guides; "dumb" failures: water hammer, blocked-outlet overpressure, backflow, exchanger rupture.

### Environment & permitting
- Major-hazard regime (hazard study, land-use planning, emergency plans), **IED / BREF** (BAT, emission limit values), EU ETS, discharges, noise; in brownfield: **notification of change** or substantial modification (a debottlenecking can reopen the whole authorisation); opposition sticking point: **technological risk and emissions**.

### Procurement
- Long-lead: **reactors, columns, compressors, furnaces (18–36 months)**, transformers, severe-service valves; vendors imposed by the licensor; shop inspection (criticality matrix), expediting; catalysts and start-up chemicals; abnormal logistics.

### Construction manager
- Heavy multidisciplinary site: **modularisation** vs stick-built, heavy lift, piping density, SIMOPS and the operator's permits, **TAR window**, productivity by discipline, mobilisation of qualified labour (welders), maximum off-site prefabrication.

### Quality (QA/QC)
- **ASME stamps / notified-body conformity**, ITP per equipment item, NDT (%), **PWHT**, systematic **PMI** in critical service, EN 10204 3.2 traceability or equivalent, hydrotests, pressure-equipment dossiers, vendor shop audits; red flag: untraced material substitution.

### Commissioning
- By systems: blowing, **chemical cleaning**, steam-out, drying, **N₂ inerting**, catalyst loading, **start-up with licensor assistance**, load increase, **performance test run** (72 h to a few days) → PAC; in brownfield: restart of the whole site as context (the project is not the only thing starting up).

### O&M (operations & maintenance)
- **RBI** (API 580 / 581), mechanical integrity, TAR plan, rotating-machine bad actors, spares, availability, energy efficiency; design for maintainability (access, lifting, isolation) reviewed from FEED.

### Grid & utility connection
- Becomes **utilities and OSBL**: steam, cooling water, air, nitrogen, flare, site electricity, effluents; in brownfield, the **available capacities** of these networks are often the real bottleneck; external electrical connection in grassroots (TSO).

### Interface manager
- **ISBL ↔ OSBL**, **licensor ↔ EPC ↔ vendors**, project ↔ operator (living site: access, permits, isolations), multiple packages, tie-ins — interface register from FEED, battery limits frozen.

### Document control
- MDR of thousands of documents, vendor data, IFR / IFA / IFC / as-built statuses, **end-of-project dossier** (pressure equipment, inspection, requalification), licensor documentation under confidentiality; the inspection dossier conditions legal operation.

## Country × sector notes (to be checked locally)

- **France**: ICPE Seveso / IED, PPRT land-use planning, DREAL, statutory pressure-equipment inspection (ministerial order), works council safety committee, prevention plan (1992 decree).
- **Belgium**: Seveso via the regional environmental permit, federal labour inspection; **Germany**: BImSchG + Störfall-Verordnung, TÜV for pressure equipment, VOB/B; **Poland / Spain**: Seveso and IED transposition, national inspection bodies (UDT in Poland, OCA in Spain).
- **United States**: OSHA PSM and EPA RMP, ASME stamps and Authorised Inspectors, API 510 / 570 / 653 in-service inspection, NEC Class / Division, state air permits (Title V), refinery-specific rules (California CalARP).
- **Gulf / India / China**: operator standards dominate (Aramco, ADNOC, IOCL, Sinopec); local inspection bodies (TSG in China, IBR in India); large greenfield complexes with modularisation and international EPC contractors.

## Cross-cutting red flags of the sector

- **Design not frozen at FID** (moving PDP, late Owner standards).
- **Tie-ins not listed and TAR window not contracted** in brownfield.
- **Long-lead items ordered after design freeze** or catalyst forgotten.
- **As-built data assumed correct** on an existing site.
- **Process guarantees falling between the licensor, the EPC and the operator** (utilities).
- **Process safety relegated behind the LTIFR** during construction on a living site.
